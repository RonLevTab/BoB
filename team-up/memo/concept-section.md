## Concept: a high-end guest-chef restaurant in Breda

**The idea.** A high-end restaurant at one fixed location in Breda where the kitchen changes hands. Every week a new guest chef takes over with a short, limited menu. Each chef brings their own music and, if they want, the personal story behind the food. The venue, the service and the quality stay the same; the chef and the menu change.

**How it works for the guest.** One ticket, one price, paid in advance: the guest chef's menu plus "pick your drink". Nobody has to work out a bill at the end of the night.

## Why this fits how people eat out now

- **People eat out less often, but spend more when they do.** In Q2 2026 Dutch consumers visited restaurants 3.8% less often than a year earlier, while spending per visit rose 1.8%. Eating out is increasingly saved for special occasions (FSIN FoodPulse, 31 Jul 2026). A limited guest-chef evening is exactly that kind of occasion.
- **A clear story wins.** The Horecava Trendreport 2026 says guests want a total experience of "atmosphere, story and emotion", and that "premium mediocre" concepts without a clear identity are losing out. The share of Millennials and Gen Z who eat out weekly halved from 50% to 25% in two years, so every visit has to count (Horecava Trendreport 2026, via Duurzaam Ondernemen, 3 Dec 2025).
- **Choice of drink matters.** Dutch restaurants such as Héron (Utrecht) report that more guests order an alcohol-free pairing even when they do not have to drink alcohol-free (Entree Magazine). Letting guests pick their drink keeps the ticket attractive to drinkers and non-drinkers alike.
- **A prepaid ticket fixes a real problem.** 88% of Dutch restaurant operators have no-shows at least monthly, but only 22% ask for prepayment (Lightspeed/Zenchef, survey of 250 operators, 13 Feb 2025). A ticket sold in advance means the guest chef cooks for a known number of guests.

## Proof that the model works

- **Carousel, London** has hosted more than 350 guest chefs since 2014, with a new chef every week at a fixed venue, the same rhythm we propose. The host provides the kitchen, staff and drinks; the guest chef brings the menu and their own followers (Carousel; Foodism, 13 Dec 2022; Club Oenologique, 3 May 2024).
- **Close to home:** "Asperges in het Veld" (2023) was a seasonal pop-up in the Breda region where 9 restaurants, including Ingenhousz Breda, took turns in one kitchen (Ingenhousz Breda).

## Why Breda

- **Residents:** 190,204 inhabitants on 1 Jan 2026, of whom 27% are aged 25-45 (CBS via AlleCijfers).
- **Students:** more than 30,000 students (BredaVandaag, 29 Aug 2025).
- **Visitors:** 5.3 million Dutch visitors in 2025, 9% more than in 2024. They came 4.1 times a year on average, against 3.2 nationally, and 70% of overnight tourists come for a city trip, event or culture (NBTC figures via Omroep Brabant, April 2026). A menu that changes gives repeat visitors a reason to come back.
- **Nothing like it yet:** Breda has fine dining (Wolfslaar, Uijttewaal, Chocolat) and ticketed dinner shows (Big Brunch Club), but our desk research found no restaurant in Breda with a rotating guest chef.

## Price positioning

In Breda, a ticketed dinner show starts at EUR 56 all-in (Big Brunch Club), a 4- to 8-course dinner at Uijttewaal costs EUR 82.50 to EUR 140, and Wolfslaar (1 Michelin star) averages about EUR 130 per person. Ticket price: **[to decide]**.

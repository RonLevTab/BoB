# Desk research: rotating guest-chef / pop-up restaurant in Breda

## Scope and status

- **Assignment:** Team up for Business (BUAS minor Building your own Business). Memo and presentation due Fri 2 Oct 2026.
- **Concept under study:** a rotating guest-chef / pop-up restaurant in Breda. A new chef takes over every week or month with a limited menu, drinks pairing and music, and tells a personal story or simply serves great food.
- **Commissioner question:** come up with new business ideas around Breda and find a location for it (see `team-up/brief.md`, updated 15 Sep 2026). This research stays neutral. It does not pick a location and it gives no recommendations. It only collects evidence.
- **Date of research:** 15 Sep 2026.
- **Method:** AI-assisted desk research (Claude, web search and page fetches), checked and curated by Ron. Every claim has a source link. Where a figure came only from a search-engine summary, or the page could not be opened, it is marked **(not verified)** or **(search summary only)**. All findings still need checking through field research: interviews with the commissioner, gemeente Breda, chefs and guests, plus a survey.
- **Reliability note:** one low-trust website (btwomrekenen.nl) claims VAT on restaurant food rose to 21% in 2026. The Belastingdienst page still lists 9% for food and non-alcoholic drinks in horeca, and Grant Thornton's Belastingplan 2026 summary names only accommodation (logies) as moving to 21%. We therefore treat the 21% claim as **wrong**.

## 1. Trends 2023-2026 (NL and international)

### 1a. Chef residencies, kitchen takeovers and pop-ups

- In London, rotating guest-chef residencies have become an established format. Carousel alone has hosted more than 300 chefs since 2014. Other venues run longer residencies: 107 Wine Shop & Bar (formerly P Franco) runs six-month residencies, and The Sea, The Sea is "turning entirely to residencies". Source: [Club Oenologique, 3 May 2024](https://cluboenologique.com/story/guest-chef-residencies-london/)
- **How the London host model works:** the host venue covers "expenses, produce, wine and provide staff". The guest chef brings the menu and their audience. As one host put it: "We've already done the expensive bit... all participating brands have to do is sell tickets." Source: [Club Oenologique, 3 May 2024](https://cluboenologique.com/story/guest-chef-residencies-london/)
- **Economics at Carousel:** guest chefs are not paid for cooking, but the host pays their travel and accommodation. The residencies are subsidised by an events space, a second restaurant and a wine bar. Co-founder Ed Templeton: "Economics are not life-changing, on either side." Source: [Foodism, 13 Dec 2022](https://foodism.co.uk/features/guest-chef-residencies/)
- **Why venues use residencies (insurance broker view):** they refresh the menu, bring in new audiences and create social-media buzz without a permanent change of concept. Source: [Howden Group UK, date not shown](https://www.howdengroup.com/uk-en/news-insights/why-chef-residencies-have-become-powerful-tool-restaurants)
- **Why chefs do residencies:** a residency lets a chef test a menu and gauge demand before committing to a restaurant, and guest chefs bring their own followers to the host venue. (search summary only) Source: [Howden Group UK](https://www.howdengroup.com/uk-en/news-insights/why-chef-residencies-have-become-powerful-tool-restaurants)
- **London pubs:** landlords rent out their kitchens to residency chefs, who pay a percentage of takings instead of start-up costs. Social media, which lets pub and chef share audiences, is named as a main driver. (search summary only, page not opened) Source: [Hot Dinners, best pop-ups and residencies, date not verified](https://www.hot-dinners.com/Features/Hot-Dinners-recommends/best-pop-ups-food-restaurants-residencies-events-london-right-now)
- **Netherlands:** an August 2026 round-up of Dutch pop-ups lists these formats:
  - Petit Péché "Sunday Summer Series" in Zandvoort: 16 Sundays with guest chefs taking over the terrace and kitchen
  - Erloom in Hilvarenbeek: long tables and open-fire cooking
  - De Maaltuin: a travelling pop-up at palaces and gardens

  Source: [Barts Boekje, 20 Aug 2026](https://www.bartsboekje.com/pop-up-restaurants-nederland/)

### 1b. Experiential and storytelling dining, local sourcing

- **Horecava Trendreport 2026:**
  - Guests want a total experience built on "atmosphere, story and emotion".
  - The share of Millennials and Gen Z who eat out weekly fell from 50% to 25% in two years.
  - "Premium mediocre" concepts without a clear identity are losing out, both to lean value concepts and to distinctive, craft- and story-led experiences.
  - Seasonal sourcing and alcohol-free drinks get equal prominence.

  Source: [Duurzaam Ondernemen on Horecava Trendreport 2026, 3 Dec 2025](https://www.duurzaam-ondernemen.nl/horecava-trendreport-2026-betaalbare-luxe-en-duurzaamheid-transformeren-de-horeca/)
- **Dutch consumers eat out less often but spend more per visit** (FSIN FoodPulse Q2 2026):
  - Visit frequency fell 3.8% year on year, while spend per visit rose 1.8%.
  - Total out-of-home spending fell 2.1%.
  - 65.5% of consumers now find eating out expensive.
  - Satisfaction with price-quality fell from 38.5% to 34.1%.
  - Restaurant visits are increasingly saved for special occasions.

  Source: [FSIN, 31 Jul 2026](https://fsin.nl/nieuws/fsin-foodpulse-nederlander-gaat-minder-vaak-uit-eten)
- FSIN estimates foodservice spending in 2025 at EUR 1,395.95 per person per year, 25.1% above 2017. (search summary only) Source: [FSIN press page](https://fsin.nl/pers)
- **Local sourcing example:** at the Blue Hill at Stone Barns residency, each resident chef sourced from the on-site farm and nearby producers. Source: [Wine Spectator, 14 Jan 2021](https://www.winespectator.com/articles/turning-tables-dan-barbers-stone-barns-restaurant-kicks-off-rotating-chef-residencies)
- No Dutch figure was found for guest demand for locally sourced menus. **(not verified)**

### 1c. Drink pairing, including non-alcoholic

- Dutch restaurants such as Héron (Utrecht) and DeZusters (Maarssen) offer full non-alcoholic pairings. Their operators report that "more and more people order it even when they do not strictly need to drink alcohol-free". The article gives no prices or demand figures. Source: [Entree Magazine, date not shown](https://www.entreemagazine.nl/dranken/creatieve-uitdaging-opvallen-met-alcoholvrije-pairings)
- **Non-alcoholic beer sales:** 7.7% more was sold in NL in H1 2026 than in H1 2025, and 22% more than in 2024. Total beer sales fell 2.8%. (search summary only, article not opened) Source: [Entree Magazine, H1 2026 beer sales](https://www.entreemagazine.nl/dranken/bierverkoop-daalt-met-28-procent-in-eerste-halfjaar-2026)
- The Nationaal Bieronderzoek 2025 reports alcohol-free speciality beer "in de lift". Source: [Misset Horeca, 2025](https://www.missethoreca.nl/412921/nationaal-bieronderzoek-2025-alcoholvrij-speciaalbier-in-de-lift)

### 1d. Ticketed / prepaid dining and no-shows

- **Lightspeed and Zenchef survey of 250 Dutch operators** (Jun-Aug 2024):
  - 88% have no-shows at least monthly, and 31% several times a week.
  - Only 22% ask for prepayment.
  - 83% send reminder emails.

  Source: [Lightspeed NL, 13 Feb 2025](https://www.lightspeedhq.nl/nieuws/no-shows/)
- **Consumer attitude:** 44% of Dutch consumers do not understand why they must pay a deposit for an online restaurant booking. (search summary only, date not verified) Source: [Misset Horeca](https://www.missethoreca.nl/396751/voorschot-voor-reservering-helft-van-gasten-snapt-niet-waarom-blijkt-uit-onderzoek)
- Operators are split on deposits: 49% think guests understand them, while 39% feel deposits do not fit hospitality. (search summary only) Source: [De RestaurantKrant](https://www.derestaurantkrant.nl/meerderheid-horecaondernemers-vraagt-liever-geen-aanbetaling-bij-reservering)
- **Tock (US ticketing platform)** claims that its customers who take deposits have an average no-show rate of 1.7%. It also says a deposit of 10% of the expected bill already cuts no-shows. This is a vendor claim; the page was blocked, so it is **(not verified)**. Source: [Tock](https://www.exploretock.com/join/resources/eliminate-no-shows-3-tock-tools/)

### 1e. Music plus food

- Restaurants built around hi-fi listening bars spread in Los Angeles in 2025. Examples are RVR and Companion (Venice), Darling by Sean Brock (West Hollywood) and The Airliner (Lincoln Heights). Stated drivers:
  - demand for "third spaces"
  - inspiration from Japanese jazz kissaten
  - a nightlife shift from dancing to listening

  Source: [Resy, 19 Nov 2025](https://blog.resy.com/2025/11/listening-bar-restaurants/)
- No Dutch data was found on demand for combined music and dining. **(not verified)**

### 1f. Social-media-driven discovery

- **Toast survey** (1,466 US adults, 9-15 Oct 2025):
  - Word of mouth is the top discovery channel overall (38%). TikTok is at 14% overall but 38% among Gen Z.
  - The main drivers of a first visit are price/value (55%) and food quality (51%).
  - This is **US data**. No Dutch equivalent was found.

  Source: [PMQ Pizza on Toast survey, 4 Jul 2026](https://www.pmq.com/how-are-guests-discovering-new-restaurants-this-national-survey-has-answers/)

### 1g. Headwinds: costs, staff, bankruptcies, spending

- **Horeca revenue (CBS):**
  - Total horeca revenue rose 3.9% in 2025, slower than the 5% of 2024.
  - Food and drink outlets grew 4.8%. Restaurants grew 3.7% in Q4 2025.
  - Horeca business confidence fell from -8.4 (Q4 2025) to -12 (Q1 2026).

  Source: [CBS, 2 Mar 2026](https://www.cbs.nl/nl-nl/nieuws/2026/10/omzet-horeca-groeit-in-vierde-kwartaal-met-ruim-3-procent)
- **ABN AMRO outlook:**
  - Revenue of food and drink outlets rose 5% in the first three quarters of 2025.
  - Closures in that period were the highest in 10 years.
  - 20% of horeca operators report problematic debts, up from about 12% in Nov 2023.
  - Purchasing power is forecast to rise 1.3% in 2026.

  Source: [Accountant.nl on ABN AMRO, 9 Jan 2026](https://www.accountant.nl/nieuws/2026/1/hogere-koopkracht-zorgt-voor-meer-bestedingen-in-horeca/)
- **Bankruptcy forecast and cost increases:** a major Dutch bank (ABN AMRO) forecast about 450 restaurant bankruptcies in 2025, against about 210 in 2023. Since 2022, food and drink producer prices rose 17-20% and CAO wages 22%. Nearly 18% of smaller horeca businesses are in a financial danger zone. Source: [OOvB, 2025](https://www.oovb.nl/nieuws/horeca-onder-druk-faillissementen-verdubbelen-2025); original bank page (timed out): [ABN AMRO](https://www.abnamro.nl/nl/zakelijk/insights/sectoren-en-trends/leisure/horeca-van-herstel-naar-crisis-aantal-faillissementen-piekt-in-2025.html)
- **Minimum wage:** the statutory minimum wage rose 22% between Dec 2022 and Jan 2024 (KHN). Source: [Entree Magazine, date not shown](https://www.entreemagazine.nl/ondernemen/khn-over-coalitieakkoord-grote-gevolgen-voor-de-horeca)
- **Staff and sector structure (KHN/ANP):**
  - Horeca has a record 522,000 jobs, and 51% of workers are aged 15-24.
  - There are nearly 44,000 horeca businesses.
  - One fifth of operators have problematic debt, and for nearly one third the viability of their business in 2026 is uncertain.

  Source: [Accountancy Vanmorgen (ANP/KHN), 12 Jan 2026](https://www.accountancyvanmorgen.nl/2026/01/12/meer-banen-dan-ooit-in-horeca-toch-zorgen-om-personeelstekort/)
- **Vacancies and training pipeline:** CBS counted 27,000 open horeca vacancies in Q1 2026. 22,000 students follow a horeca programme in 2025-26, about 6,000 fewer than 10 years ago. (search summary only) Source: [KHN, "Zelfde cijfers, ander verhaal"](https://khn.nl/nieuws/zelfde-cijfers-ander-verhaal)
- **VAT in 2026 and 2027:**
  - Food and non-alcoholic drinks in horeca stay at 9%. Source: [Belastingdienst, undated page](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/zakelijk/btw/tarieven_en_vrijstellingen/goederen_9_btw/voedingsmiddelen/)
  - Alcoholic drinks are at 21% (beer above 0.5% ABV, other drinks above 1.2% ABV). (search summary only) Source: [Moore DRV](https://www.moore-drv.nl/btw-tarieven-alcohol/)
  - VAT on accommodation (logies) went from 9% to 21% on 1 Jan 2026. The 9% rate for culture, media and sport was kept. No 2027 horeca VAT change was listed. Source: [Grant Thornton, 17 Sep 2025](https://www.grantthornton.nl/insights/themas/prinsjesdag/2025/belastingplan-2026-maatregelen-btw-accijnzen/)
- **Alcohol excise:**
  - A planned one-off 2.6% inflation correction for 2026 was dropped. (search summary only) Source: [biernet.nl](https://www.biernet.nl/blog/bieraccijns-en-belastingwijzigingen-wat-betekent-dit-voor-jou)
  - In April 2026 the cabinet proposed automatic annual indexation of alcohol excise from 2027. The average effect is about 1.5 cent more per glass by 2032, and about EUR 130 million a year in extra revenue after roughly 5 years. Source: [STAP, 20 Apr 2026](https://www.stap.nl/nl/nieuws/laatste-nieuws.html/3454/10675/kabinet-stelt-voor-accijnzen-op-alcohol-automatisch-te-indexeren)
  - KHN opposes the proposal because of margin pressure. Source: [Misset Horeca, 22 Apr 2026](https://www.missethoreca.nl/415707/khn-accijnsverhoging-op-alcohol-is-slecht-idee)
  - Whether the proposal has passed parliament: **(not verified)**.
- **Self-employment enforcement:** full enforcement against false self-employment (Wet DBA) started on 1 Jan 2025, with fines and back-tax claims possible. This matters if guest chefs are hired as zzp'ers. Source: [Rijksoverheid, 6 Sep 2024](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2024/09/06/vanaf-1-januari-2025-volledige-handhaving-op-schijnzelfstandigheid)

## 3. Breda market

### Population and age

- Breda had 190,204 inhabitants on 1 Jan 2026 (CBS data). Age structure:

  | Age | Inhabitants | Share |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0-15 | 26,891 | 14% |
  | 15-25 | 25,888 | 14% |
  | 25-45 | 51,685 | 27% |
  | 45-65 | 48,089 | 25% |
  | 65+ | 37,651 | 20% |

- There were 92,225 households in 2025, with an average size of 2.02. Average income per income earner was EUR 44,700 in 2024.

  Source: [AlleCijfers (CBS data), updated 7 Sep 2026](https://allecijfers.nl/gemeente/breda/). Official dashboard: [Breda in Cijfers](https://breda.incijfers.nl/dashboard/bevolking)

### Students

- Breda has more than 30,000 students and welcomed more than 10,000 new students in 2025-26. Institutions: Avans, BUas, St. Joost, Curio, De Rooi Pannen and KMA. Each year about 300 hbo and 500 mbo graduates settle in Breda. Source: [BredaVandaag, 29 Aug 2025](https://www.bredavandaag.nl/algemeen/algemeen/455997/breda-verwelkomt-10-000-nieuwe-studenten)
- Avans Breda had 14,040 students on 1 Dec 2024 (11,178 full-time, 2,622 part-time, 240 dual). Source: [Avans in cijfers](https://www.avans.nl/over-avans/organisatie/avans-in-cijfers)
- BUas has "more than 7,000 students from some 100 countries". The page is undated, and a current exact figure is **not verified**. Source: [BUas About us](https://www.buas.nl/en/about-us)

### Visitors and tourism

- **Visitors (NBTC figures, presented by Breda Marketing):**
  - More than 5.3 million Dutch visitors came to Breda in 2025, 9% more than in 2024.
  - The average visitor came 4.1 times a year, up from 3.4 in 2024. The national average is 3.2.
  - 70% of overnight tourists come for a city trip, an event or culture.

  Source: [Omroep Brabant, April 2026](https://www.omroepbrabant.nl/nieuws/6006220/breda-populair-bij-toeristen-vaker-herhaalbezoek). The page shows 4 Apr without a clear year; 2026 is inferred because the article reports full-year 2025 data.
- The 2019 level was 6.131 million visitors, so 2025 is still below pre-pandemic. Source: [BredaVandaag, April 2026](https://www.bredavandaag.nl/algemeen/algemeen/471943/aantal-bezoekers-breda-neemt-toe-9-procent-meer-toeristen-in-2025)
- The number of overnight stays in Breda was not found. **(not verified)**
- Breda tourist tax for hotels is 3.50% of the room price. Source: [Verordening toeristenbelasting Breda 2025](https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR729530/)

### Horeca supply and city policy

- **Nota "Kiezen voor kwaliteit"** (Breda's horeca policy, in force since 20 Aug 2025):
  - Breda had 583 commercial horeca businesses in Oct 2024, or 3.1 per 1,000 inhabitants and 22% more than 10 years earlier. Fast service and restaurants have declined slightly since 2021.
  - Breda has relatively many horeca businesses compared with benchmark cities.
  - The city aims for "quality, diversity and innovation" and is "conservative on quantity".
  - A new horeca business must show both need and added value at its specific location.
  - Pop-ups are mentioned as a way to bring vitality to the Stationsgebied.
  - Up to 10 times a year, longer opening hours until 06:00 are possible for night culture.
  - The Nota has no dedicated section on temporary or pop-up horeca rules.

  Source: [Nota Kiezen voor kwaliteit, lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl](https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR743429/1)
- The number of horeca businesses inside the city centre alone was not found. **(not verified)**
- Breda-specific eating-out spending was not found. Only the national FSIN figure is available (see 1b). **(not verified)**

## 4. Feasibility and regulation (NL / Breda)

### Alcohol and horeca permits

- **Which permit:**
  - Serving alcohol in horeca requires an **Alcoholwetvergunning**.
  - An **exploitatievergunning** is only needed for alcohol-free horeca in certain Breda streets (check the street list), and in every street for coffeeshops and similar.

  Sources: [Gemeente Breda, Horecavergunning aanvragen](https://www.breda.nl/horecavergunning-aanvragen); [Stratenoverzicht exploitatievergunning](https://www.breda.nl/stratenoverzicht-exploitatievergunning)
- **National requirements for every owner and leidinggevende** (person in charge) on an alcohol licence: at least 21 years old, a Sociale Hygiëne diploma or certificate, and registration in the Register Sociale Hygiëne. Source: [Ondernemersplein (KVK/Rijksoverheid), reviewed 1 Aug 2025](https://ondernemersplein.overheid.nl/alcohol-verkopen/)
- **Conduct check:** Breda screens the conduct (levensgedrag) of the operator and every leidinggevende. It checks justice records, police reports and inspector files. The same check applies when a leidinggevende is added or changed, and for temporary alcohol permits. Source: [Gemeente Breda, Levensgedrag](https://www.breda.nl/levensgedrag-en-aanvraag-vergunning)
- **Breda fees (leges) 2026:**

  | Item | Fee |
  |---|---|
  | Alcoholwet licence | EUR 598.19 |
  | Change of leidinggevende | EUR 318.12 |
  | Artikel 35 temporary exemption | EUR 150.29 |
  | Exploitatievergunning | EUR 604.75 |
  | Event permit, category A | EUR 400.00 |
  | Event permit, category B | EUR 4,877.68 |
  | Event permit, category C | EUR 17,297.36 |

  Source: [Legesverordening Breda 2026](https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR750749)
- **Artikel 35 temporary exemption (tapontheffing):**
  - Covers low-alcohol drinks only (below 15% ABV), at a special occasion "of very temporary nature".
  - Maximum 12 consecutive days, under supervision of people who meet the age and Sociale Hygiëne requirements.
  - Typically applied for at least 10 working days in advance.

  These conditions come from national rules and other municipalities' policy rules. Breda's own conditions are behind a DigiD/eHerkenning login and are **not verified**. Sources: [Ondernemersplein](https://ondernemersplein.overheid.nl/alcohol-verkopen/); [example policy rule art. 35, lokaleregelgeving](https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR708071); [Breda, Alcohol tijdelijk schenken](https://www.breda.nl/alcohol-tijdelijk-schenken-aanvragen)
- **Rotating operators:** it is **not verified** whether a guest chef who cooks but does not run the bar must be listed as leidinggevende on the host's licence. Ask gemeente Breda.

### Events

- **Notification instead of a permit (melding):** small events can be notified rather than permitted when all of these apply:
  - at most 300 people at the same time
  - at most 3 days
  - between 08:00 and 23:00 (Sundays 13:00-23:00)
  - at most 65 dB(A) at the nearest noise-sensitive building
  - notified at least 4 weeks ahead

  **This notification route does not apply inside the defined city-centre area, where a permit is required.** Source: [Besluit meldingsplichtige evenementen en buurtfeesten Breda 2022, in force 29 Dec 2022](https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR688532/1)
- **Reservation calendar:** events go through Breda's reservation calendar, where the municipality assesses them within about 2 weeks. Registering before 1 Oct of the previous year avoids precario tax. Transition rule: events in Jan-Mar 2027 must register by 1 Aug 2026, and events in Apr-Dec 2027 by 1 Oct 2026. Source: [Gemeente Breda, Evenement organiseren](https://www.breda.nl/evenement-organiseren)

### Food safety

- Every business that prepares food must work with a HACCP food safety plan (EU Regulation 852/2004). The NVWA-approved **KHN Hygiënecode voor de horeca** meets this obligation. The NVWA checks both the plan and how it is applied. Source: [NVWA, Hygiënecode](https://www.nvwa.nl/onderwerpen/voedselveilig-werken-in-horeca-ambacht-en-retail/hygienecode)
- **Allergens:** horeca must be able to inform guests about 14 allergens, in writing or verbally. If done verbally, a visible sign must say guests can ask. Source: [NVWA, Allergenen in horeca](https://www.nvwa.nl/onderwerpen/voedselveiligheid/voedselveilig-werken-in-horeca-ambacht-en-retail/hoe-werk-ik-veilig/allergenen)
- Since 2021, allergen information has been a fixed part of NVWA horeca inspections. (search summary only) Source: [NVWA, Allergenen bij onverpakte levensmiddelen](https://www.nvwa.nl/onderwerpen/voedselveiligheid/allergenen/allergenen-bij-onverpakte-levensmiddelen)
- **Rotating menus:** a new menu every week or month means allergen information must be updated each time. This follows from the rule above and should be checked with a food-safety adviser.

### Music licensing

- Background music in horeca needs a **combined BumaStemra plus Sena licence**, arranged through mijnlicentie.nl. Source: [BumaStemra](https://bumastemra.nl/licentie/licenties-voor-achtergrond-muziek/)
- **Sena 2026 horeca background music** (Sena share only):

  | Floor area | Base rate | After discount |
  |---|---|---|
  | Up to 100 m2 | EUR 1,011.43 | EUR 674.32 |
  | 101-200 m2 | EUR 1,427.99 | EUR 952.04 |

- **Sena amusement music at events:** if the venue owner organises an event with a ticket above EUR 25 excl. VAT, the tariff is 1.625% of box-office takings. An external organiser in a rented venue needs a separate Sena event licence.

  Source: [Sena Tarievenoverzicht 2026 (PDF)](https://sena.nl/assets/tarieven/sena_tarievenenoverzicht_2026.pdf)
- BumaStemra horeca tariff for 31-100 m2: EUR 672.00 per year, or EUR 448.02 after the standard discount, excl. VAT. Sena comes on top. (search summary only, year not verified) Source: [BumaStemra](https://bumastemra.nl/licentie/licenties-voor-achtergrond-muziek/)

### Insurance, liability and contracts

- An insurance broker advises that public and product liability cover should extend to guest chefs and any staff they bring. For ticketed events, event cover and contractual responsibilities should also be reviewed. This is UK context; Dutch policy wording is **not verified**. Source: [Howden Group UK](https://www.howdengroup.com/uk-en/news-insights/why-chef-residencies-have-become-powerful-tool-restaurants)
- **Guest chefs as zzp'ers:** full Wet DBA enforcement has applied since 1 Jan 2025, with back taxes and fines possible. Source: [Rijksoverheid, 6 Sep 2024](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2024/09/06/vanaf-1-januari-2025-volledige-handhaving-op-schijnzelfstandigheid)
- Horeca advisers say that fixed hours, following instructions and cooking a set menu are signals of false self-employment. (lower-trust commercial source) Source: [Mr Einstein](https://mreinstein.nl/wet-dba-2025/)

## Gaps to validate with field research

These are questions desk research could not answer. Use them as the basis for interview and survey questions.

1. **Commissioner:** what is the exact question, and what is the commissioner's role (venue owner, chef, investor, city)? Which constraints are fixed: budget, location, timeline?
2. **Willingness to pay in Breda:** what will Breda guests pay for a ticketed guest-chef dinner, with and without an alcoholic or non-alcoholic pairing? Local Breda pop-up or tasting-menu prices are largely missing.
3. **Target segment:** which group responds most? Students (30,000+), residents aged 25-45 (27% of the population), or day and overnight visitors (5.3 million a year)?
4. **Frequency:** is a new chef every week or every month preferred? How often would one guest return?
5. **Prepayment:** will Breda guests accept buying tickets in advance, given that 44% of Dutch consumers nationally do not understand deposits?
6. **Guest-chef supply:** which chefs in Breda and West-Brabant would take part? What do they want (fee, revenue share, exposure, test kitchen)? No Dutch data exists on host and chef deal terms.
7. **Discovery channels in NL:** how do Breda diners find new restaurants (Instagram, TikTok, word of mouth)? The Toast data is US-only.
8. **Local demand for pairing and music:** is there interest in non-alcoholic pairing and in music (DJ, vinyl, live) during dinner in Breda?
9. **Competition:** is there an existing supper club, chef's table or guest-chef series in Breda? Why did INTI Gastrobar close, and when? How many horeca businesses are in the city centre?
10. **Premises:** which vacant city-centre units have horeca zoning? Would landlords accept a temporary lease? Does gemeente Breda see a rotating concept as "added value" under the Nota "Kiezen voor kwaliteit"? Is there a meanwhile-use route for horeca?
11. **Licensing route (interview gemeente Breda, vergunningen):**
    - Whose name goes on the Alcoholwet licence when chefs rotate?
    - Do guest chefs need a conduct (levensgedrag) check or registration as leidinggevende?
    - Are the Breda-specific artikel 35 conditions and repeat limits workable?
    - Which event rules apply inside the city-centre area?
12. **Employment and tax (interview an accountant):** can guest chefs work as zzp'ers under Wet DBA? How is VAT split on all-in tickets (food 9%, alcohol 21%)? Does 2027 excise indexation affect pricing?
13. **Insurance (interview a Dutch insurer or broker):** does liability cover extend to guest chefs and their staff? Is event or cancellation cover needed?
14. **Food safety for rotating menus:** how is HACCP and allergen compliance guaranteed when the chef changes every week or month?
15. **Music costs:** what is the actual combined BumaStemra plus Sena cost for a specific floor area and format (background music vs DJ or live)?
16. **Tourism and spending data:** number of overnight stays in Breda, and local eating-out spend and frequency. Neither was found.
17. **Belgian and Nordic comparables:** a rotating guest-chef venue in Antwerp, Ghent or Copenhagen was not verified.
18. **Front-of-house staff:** are service staff available in Breda (for example BUas hospitality students), given national shortages and 27,000 open horeca vacancies?
